Last spring, our neighborhood association decided to turn an abandoned lot into a community garden. We cobbled together a few grants, borrowed tools from whoever would lend them, and spent every Saturday digging.

The first harvest was modest, to put it mildly — a handful of radishes and a lot of very determined weeds. Still, by August the place had become a meeting point, and people who had lived on the same street for years were finally on first-name terms.
