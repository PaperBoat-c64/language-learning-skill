# German — Teaching Norm

Load when `textLanguage` is `de`. German marks case, gender, number, and person; word order is grammatical, not stylistic; separable verbs and compounds are central.

## 1. Orthography and Unicode

- Umlauts `ä ö ü` are distinct letters. Never replace with `ae oe ue` in quoted text; the digraph spelling occurs in names, crosswords, and some Swiss usage. Preserve what the source uses.
- `ß` (Eszett) appears after long vowels/diphthongs (`Straße`, `heißen`); `ss` after short vowels (`Fluss`, `dass`). Swiss German uses `ss` throughout. Do not "modernize" `ß` to `ss`.
- All nouns are capitalized. Keep capitalization in notes and examples.
- Quotation marks: `„ “` (German), `« »` (Swiss/French-influenced), `" "` (informal). Preserve.
- Hyphens and dashes function differently; keep `-`, `–`, `—` distinct.

## 2. Segmentation

Split at `.` `?` `!` and `…`. Do not split after:

- `z. B.`, `d. h.`, `u. a.`, `usw.`, `bzw.`, `ca.`, `Nr.`, `Dr.`, `Prof.`, `St.`, `Abs.`, `Art.`, `S.`;
- ordinals with a period: `am 3. Mai`, `der 1. Platz`;
- abbreviations in addresses and times (`10.30 Uhr`);
- numbers: `3,14`, `1.000,50`.

Note that German sentences can have very long verb-final subordinate clauses; prefer the larger unit.

## 3. Nouns, gender, case, articles

- Three genders: masculine, feminine, neuter. Gender is lexical; compounds take the gender of the last element (`der Vorderreifen`, `die Haustür`, `das Fahrrad`).
- Four cases: nominative, accusative, dative, genitive. Case is assigned by verb, preposition, or syntactic function.
- Definite article: `der/die/das` (nom.), `den/die/das` (acc.), `dem/der/dem` (dat.), `des/der/des` (gen.).
- Indefinite: `ein/eine/ein`; negative `kein/keine/kein`.
- Adjective declension: weak (after definite article), mixed (after indefinite), strong (no article). Identify which pattern and why. Example: `mit dem alten Fahrrad` (dative, weak).
- Plurals: `-e`, `-er`, `-en`, `-s`, umlaut-only, zero. Record the plural class for annotated nouns.
- Genitive: `des Mannes`, `der Frau`; colloquial replacement with `von + dat.` is common — note register if it appears.
- Article contractions: `zum` = `zu + dem`, `zur` = `zu + der`, `im` = `in + dem`, `am` = `an + dem`, `ins` = `in + das`, `ans` = `an + das`, `vom` = `von + dem`, `beim` = `bei + dem`.

## 4. Verbs

- Weak, strong, and mixed verbs. Strong verbs change stem and participle (`singen – sang – gesungen`).
- Tenses: present (also future with time expression), preterite (narrative/written), perfect (`haben`/`sein` + participle, spoken past), pluperfect, future I/II.
- `sein` as perfect auxiliary with intransitive motion/change verbs and `sein`/`bleiben`/`werden`.
- Separable verbs: prefix moves to the clause end in main clauses (`ich kriege es hin`), stays attached in subordinate clauses and with modals (`…, dass ich es hinkriege`; `ich will es hinkriegen`).
- Inseparable prefixes (`be-, ge-, er-, ver-, zer-, ent-, emp-, miss-`) never separate and have no `ge-` in the participle.
- Participles: weak `ge-…-t`, strong `ge-…-en`; separable `auf-ge-pumpt`; inseparable `verstanden`.
- Konjunktiv II: `würde + inf` for most verbs; synthetic forms for `sein`, `haben`, modals (`wäre`, `hätte`, `könnte`). Used for hypotheses, politeness, unreal conditions.
- Passive: `werden + participle` (process) vs. `sein + participle` (state). Distinguish.
- Modal verbs: `können, müssen, dürfen, sollen, wollen, mögen`; `müssen` + negation often = "not need to" in scope, not "must not" (`nicht müssen` vs. `nicht dürfen`).
- `lassen` + inf; `brauchen … zu`; `scheinen … zu`.

## 5. Word order

- Main clause: finite verb in second position (V2). Subject–verb inversion when something else occupies first position: `Zum Glück kam meine Nachbarin`.
- Subordinate clause: finite verb final (`…, die so etwas repariert`; `Als ich … holte, …`).
- Questions: yes/no → verb first; wh-questions → wh-word first, verb second.
- TeKaMoLo: temporal, causal, modal, local ordering of adverbials as a default tendency, not a rigid rule.
- Negation: `nicht` placement depends on scope; `kein` for indefinite nouns.
- Modal particles (`doch`, `mal`, `ja`, `halt`, `eben`, `wohl`) are near-untranslatable stance markers; gloss their function.

## 6. Prepositions and case government

Record as valency:

- accusative: `durch, für, gegen, ohne, um, bis, entlang`;
- dative: `aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu, gegenüber`;
- genitive: `wegen, trotz, während, statt` (colloquially dative — note register);
- two-way (`Wechselpräpositionen`): `in, an, auf, über, unter, vor, hinter, neben, zwischen` — accusative for direction, dative for location.
- Verb government: `warten auf + Akk.`, `sich freuen auf/über`, `abhängen von + Dat.`, `sich interessieren für + Akk.`, `teilnehmen an + Dat.`, `sich erinnern an + Akk.`
- Adjective government: `stolz auf + Akk.`, `zufrieden mit + Dat.`, `abhängig von + Dat.`

## 7. Compounds

- German compounds are written as one word and take the gender of the final element.
- Split transparent compounds in glosses: `Vorderreifen` = `Vorder-` (front) + `Reifen` (tire); `Küchenboden` = `Küche` + `Boden`; `Handumdrehen` is lexicalized (idiom).
- Linking elements (`-s-`, `-n-`, `-e-`) have no single rule; note them, do not invent one.
- Do not treat a transparent compound as an idiom. Do treat a lexicalized compound with non-compositional meaning (`Feierabend`, `Fernweh`) as a vocabulary item with a note.

## 8. Register and address

- `du` (informal singular), `ihr` (informal plural), `Sie` (formal, capitalized). The choice is social; note it when the text turns on it.
- Colloquial: `kriegen` (get), `kaputt`, `platt` (flat/tired), `Kram`, `Sachen`; contractions in speech (`hab', is'`).
- Swear words and intensity particles (`verdammt`, `Scheiße`, `Mist`) — mark sensitivity; do not recommend productive use.
- Officialese and academic prose use nominalizations and long attributes; explain them, do not imitate them.

## 9. Regional variation

- Austria: `heuer` (this year), `Jause`, `Erdäpfel`, `Sackerl`, `topfen`; different administrative vocabulary.
- Switzerland: no `ß`; `Velo`, `Natel`, `Guetzli`; different official terms.
- Bavaria/southern Germany: `Grüß Gott`, `Semmel`, `servus`.
- Northern Germany: `Moin`, `Sonnabend`.
- Label region only with evidence from the source, a cited dictionary, or unmistakable institutional vocabulary. Otherwise `regionCertainty: "unverified"`.

## 10. Common learner errors and false friends

- `bekommen` = to get, not "to become" (`werden`).
- `Gift` = poison, not "gift".
- `bald` = soon, not "bald".
- `also` = therefore/so, not "also" (`auch`).
- `wer` = who, `wo` = where, `was` = what — interrogatives differ from English.
- Wrong case after two-way prepositions; forgetting adjective endings; V2 violations in main clauses; verb not final in subordinate clauses; separable prefix left attached in main clauses.
- `nicht` vs. `kein`; `sein` vs. `haben` as perfect auxiliary.
- Confusing `dass` (that) and `das` (the/which).
- Translating modal particles literally.

## 11. Teaching priorities for German texts

1. Case and adjective declension in the actual phrase.
2. Separable verbs and the clause bracket.
3. Verb-final word order in subordinate clauses.
4. Preposition + case government.
5. Compound decomposition.
6. Modal particles and register.
